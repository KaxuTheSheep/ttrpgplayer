Disguise represents a creature's ability to pass as someone or something else through outfit, mannerism, and performance rather than through concealment or shadow. It produces the **Disguised** [[Conditions|condition]], tracked individually per observer — you may be convincingly Disguised to one creature and seen through by another simultaneously. Unlike [[Stealth]], Disguise is not about being unseen. You are in full view. The lie is *who you appear to be*.

## Assuming a Disguise
You may attempt to assume a Disguise if you have a plausible outfit, role, or cover identity to present — enemy colours, a servant's livery, a forged token, or simply the practiced bearing of someone you are not.

Make a **[[Skills|Performance]] Test**. The result becomes your **Disguise DN** against each observer who has not already met you in another identity, until something ends it (below).

## Detection
Any observer who has reason to look closely may attempt an **[[Attributes|Insight]] Test** against your Disguise DN, either on their own turn or at GM discretion for passive suspicion (a checkpoint guard's routine glance, for instance).
- **Success:** that observer sees through you. Disguised ends *for them specifically* — others who haven't rolled, or who failed, may still be fooled.
- **Failure:** they remain convinced.

Detection here is a matter of judgement and instinct rather than raw perception — this is why Disguise uses [[Attributes|Insight]] rather than [[Skills|Perception]].

## Maintaining Disguised
- **[[Movement Types|Running]], fighting, or otherwise drawing attention does not end Disguised** on its own — a disguise is not broken by being seen, only by being seen through.
- **Breaking character** is the primary risk: acting in a way inconsistent with the identity you're presenting (a "servant" giving orders, a "guard" who doesn't know the password, hesitating on a detail your cover should know cold). When you do, the GM may call for an immediate opposed [[Attributes|Insight]] Test from any observer who witnessed the slip, comparing against your Disguise DN, rather than waiting for a passive check.
- Convincing props, correct passwords, or a well-prepared cover story may grant Advantage on the initial Performance Test or on Tests to recover from a slip, at GM discretion.

## Acting In Disguise
Acting under a Disguise does not inherently grant Advantage or deny [[Dodge]]/[[Parry]] the way attacking from [[Stealth]] does — Disguise conceals identity, not position or intent. Its value is social and situational: walking somewhere you shouldn't, extracting information, or setting up a moment where being unmasked matters far less than being believed until then.

## Re-Establishing a Disguise
If a Disguise is seen through by an observer, you cannot simply re-attempt it against that same observer — they know your face now, cover or not. You may still attempt a **new** Disguise (different identity, different outfit) against them from scratch, at the GM's discretion for plausibility, but the old cover is burned for that observer permanently for the scene.

## Group Detection
Track Disguised separately per observer, the same as [[Stealth]]. A GM may simplify this for large crowds by rolling once for "the checkpoint" or "the camp" as a group where individual tracking isn't meaningful to the scene.
